Who I Work With

Different CEA businesses have different operational problems.

The common thread is usually the same: there's a gap between how the operation was expected to perform and what's actually happening in the real world. My role is helping identify where that gap is coming from and what it will realistically take to improve it.

01 — CEA Operators

CEA Operators

If your facility isn't performing the way the model said it would, the hardest part is usually figuring out why. Yield, labor efficiency, energy use, workflow bottlenecks, food safety systems, and software infrastructure all affect each other. Most operations don't have a single problem — they have several overlapping ones.

  • Operations and unit economics assessments
  • Production economics and crop profitability modeling
  • Technology and systems selection
  • Scale-up planning
  • SOP and operational process development

Operations Assessment

Structured review of production systems, labor performance, workflow, supply chain, energy usage, and operating costs. The goal is to identify where operational assumptions are breaking down and which problems are actually worth solving first.

Production Economics Modeling

Analysis of crop profitability, throughput, labor efficiency, and production costs using real operating assumptions instead of generic benchmarks.

Systems and Technology Selection

Evaluation of climate systems, automation platforms, SCADA, WMS, WOMS, and operational software based on the actual needs of the facility — not just vendor specifications.

Scale-Up Planning

Support for facilities moving from pilot to commercial scale, where operational problems often change dramatically as throughput increases.

SOPs and Data Discipline

Clear operating procedures and reliable production data are foundational to improving performance. Most facilities struggle because they either don't have the right data or can't trust the numbers they're collecting.

02 — Equipment Vendors

Equipment Vendors

Many CEA implementation problems happen because the operator and the vendor are solving different parts of the same challenge. Vendors understand their systems deeply. Operators are still learning what their operation needs to support those systems successfully. Bridging that gap earlier usually leads to cleaner integrations, fewer delays, and better long-term outcomes.

  • Operator-side support during pre-sales discussions
  • Integration readiness and workflow analysis
  • Operational planning around new systems
  • Support during implementation and commissioning
  • Added operational credibility during the sales process

Pre-Sales Advisory

Helping operators understand how equipment choices affect staffing, workflows, production planning, and operational infrastructure before purchase decisions are finalized.

Integration Readiness

Identifying operational gaps before implementation begins — upstream/downstream workflow impacts, staffing requirements, software dependencies, and ownership questions.

Implementation Support

Working alongside vendor and operator teams during deployment to help keep operational requirements aligned with project execution.

Sales Process Support

Providing operators with an experienced operator-side perspective during evaluation and purchasing conversations.

03 — Investors & Lenders

Investors & Lenders

Most CEA financial models look reasonable on paper. The challenge is whether the operational assumptions behind those models will actually hold up once the facility starts running. That's difficult to evaluate without experience inside live operations.

  • Pro forma and unit economics review
  • Facility feasibility analysis
  • Operational due diligence
  • Technology and systems review
  • Post-investment operational assessment

Pro Forma Stress Testing

Review of production assumptions, labor models, ramp timelines, energy usage, throughput expectations, and operating costs against real-world facility performance.

Feasibility Review

Assessment of whether the proposed crop mix, technology stack, staffing plan, and facility scale are likely to produce the projected economics.

Operations Assessment

Evaluation of existing facilities against projected performance — including yield, labor efficiency, operating costs, food safety systems, and workflow design.

Technology Review

Independent review of operational systems, automation, software platforms, and infrastructure to identify integration or scalability concerns.

Unit Economics Analysis

Understanding where cost and performance gaps are coming from, which problems are structural, and which can realistically be improved operationally.
