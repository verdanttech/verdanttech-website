Who I Work With

Scaling CEA introduces challenges that look different depending on who you are.

The complexity is real whether you're running the operation, selling into it, or financing it, and you are going to feel it in different ways.

Operators

The gap between where your operation is and where it needs to be — on costs, yield, and quality — is real, but where it's coming from isn't always obvious. Scaling up introduces decisions about systems, equipment, and operational models you may not have encountered before.

You need clarity.

  • A diagnosis, not another hypothesis about where margin is going
  • A cost model that reflects what the operation actually does — not what was projected at build
  • A clear read on which systems gaps are fixable and what closing them realistically requires
  • A scale-up plan built around what actually breaks at production volume
Vendors

By the time equipment arrives on site, gaps between the operator's planning assumptions and the reality of implementation are already baked in. The downstream costs — delays, change orders, support burdens — fall on the vendor as much as the operator.

You need to get it right for your customer.

  • A customer who arrives at integration knowing what their operation actually requires — before the installation begins
  • A pre-sales conversation that demonstrates operational depth your competitors can't match
  • An operator-side diagnosis when a post-install engagement starts going sideways — not a support queue
Investors

CEA operations are complex enough that standard due diligence doesn't always surface the right questions. The numbers in the model may be honest — and still not reflect what the facility will actually do at production scale.

You want an independent read.

  • A clear view of which pro forma assumptions are defensible and which ones the facility will struggle to meet — before capital is committed
  • An operational assessment from someone with no stake in the outcome
  • A picture of whether the operation is performing against its model and where the gaps are — while there's still time to act

The unit costs and production targets of most CEA facilities were set with models built before the farm had real data to work with. Call it Spreadsheet Farming — projections based on assumed yield, estimated labor efficiency, and raw materials and energy consumption forcasts from the best information available at the time. The models are useful for early-stage decision making, but they're usually out of date on launch day and don't age well from there. The farm keeps running, but the model often stays frozen with its original assumptions and the gap between what the spreadsheet said and what the operation can perform is where margin disappears, investor expectations go unmet, and operators run out of answers.

Performance Tracking and Data Discipline

One of the reasons performance gaps are hard to close is that often the data needed to diagnose them doesn't exist or is hard to compile. Most facilities struggle with this. Production SOPs are there, but tracking compliance is spotty at best. Food safety plans are in place, but records are in binders and hard to review. Data collection infrastructure is what's needed, but lack of easy record creates exposure to audit failure, buyer relationship risk from missed projections, and management decisions made on bad numbers. I'll help you identify and implement tools to streamline your data collection workload and improve your visibility into the intelligence the data has to offer.

Pre-Investment Analysis

Pro Forma Stress-Testing

I'll give you a line-by-line review of yield assumptions, production cost structures, energy sensitivity, and labor models. A particular focus I'll look at is the ramp period — the gap between when a facility starts operating and when it reaches its projected throughput. It's one of the most consistently undermodeled risks in CEA pro formas. My starting point is identifying which numbers are defensible against actual operating experience and which require assumptions the facility will struggle to meet. The industry has a Spreadsheet Farming problem: capital gets committed against projections built on assumed inputs, and without actual operating data feeding back into those models over time, the gaps between projections and performance compound silently until they becomes capital problems. I'll help find the gaps.

Feasibility and Pre-Development Review

For facilities still in design or early development, I can assess the proposed growing systems, crops, and technology support stack.
